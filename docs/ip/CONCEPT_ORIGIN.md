# Concept origin

> **Not legal advice.** Engineering record of where each new concept comes from and
> what was deliberately excluded. Publication requires Turkish IP counsel sign-off.

The owner supplied reference screenshots of three third-party products for this wave
(2026-09-23). They were viewed once, at contact-sheet size, to identify the **product
category** only. They are not stored in this repository and are not given to any
model with an instruction to reproduce them. Nothing was traced, OCR'd, measured or
extracted from them. The references are not sources and are not credited.

Each entry lists (1) the abstract product problem taken, which is an idea or genre
convention and not protectable expression, and (2) the expression explicitly excluded.

## A. Apartment community and maintenance game (working name private)

**Abstract problems taken**

- Make the life of a building and its residents visible at a glance.
- Present open problems as concrete, actionable decisions.
- Let a player grow from one building's concerns outwards over time.

**TarikLab's own premise.** A building's *social memory and maintenance debt*.
Residents are a network with trust, tolerance for disruption, needs and ties to each
other. Short-term cash comfort competes with the building's long-term health,
relationships, safety and reputation. The city is not something to conquer. It is
suppliers, municipal services, environmental risk and joint projects, all deferred
until the single-building slice proves itself.

**Excluded expression**

- No vertical cut-away as the main scene. The first slice uses a top-down
  *courtyard plan* (avlu planı): wings around a shared court, stairwells and a
  plant room.
- No mascot, character art, building facades, card frames, icon set, colour system,
  tutorial text, mission economy, currency names, reward loop, city-growth map or
  "take over the neighbour" mechanic from the reference.
- No freemium visual idiom: no saturated candy palette, no coin bursts, no timers
  to wait out.
- All resident names, problem texts and outcomes are written from scratch for
  TarikLab.

## B. Transit-network game (working name private; "Akış Hatları" is internal only)

**Abstract problems taken**

- A readable network made from a few simple building actions.
- Capacity pressure that rises gradually.
- Depth from a small toolset.

These are genre-level ideas shared by many network and logistics games.

**TarikLab's own premise.** A fictional coastal city's *urban rhythm network*.

- The player serves need flows (work, care, school, health, night life) across
  time-of-day windows.
- Four modes are each bound by terrain: night ferries cross water, slope lifts climb
  hills, neighbourhood trams run on the flat, and footbridges span short gaps.
- District agreements are commitments with visible, delayed consequences.
- Play is **turn-based by rhythm window**, not real time.
- Success is measured by rhythm coverage, transfer reliability, district fragility
  and accessibility, not by passengers delivered before overflow.

**Excluded expression**

- No white or pale background with bright multi-colour lines.
- No geometric-shape stations or passengers (circle, triangle, square…).
- No real-city river map.
- No weekly upgrade pick, no "carriage/locomotive/tunnel" inventory.
- No overflow-timer loss condition, no clock-dial UI, no minimal sans-serif
  map-poster look.
- No sound design (TarikLab games are silent).
- Never described or marketed as similar to any existing game.

## C. Interactive novella (working series name private)

**Abstract problems taken**

- Atmospheric chapter transitions.
- A feeling of physical reading.
- Choices embedded in the flow of prose.
- Strong content hierarchy.

The interactive-book form itself is centuries old and free to use.

**TarikLab's own premise.**

- The first slice is **"Arşiv Odası"** (working story title). A municipal archive
  clerk finds a file about a demolished building that the records say never
  existed.
- It has a start, three meaningful choices and two endings.
- The metaphor is an **archive folder and index cards**.

**Excluded expression**

- No leather-bound book, no red cover, no page-curl.
- No chapter-card layout or choice-link styling copied from any reference.
- No characters, events, lines or chapter names from any other work.
- No public-domain or translated text, no illustrations, no narration, no music.
